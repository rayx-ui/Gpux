# Positioning And Scroll State

## Purpose And Usage

Read before changing fixed/sticky behavior, sticky candidates in `div`/`list`/`uniform_list`, positioned or top-layer ordering, `ScrollHandle`, `ScrollActivity`, or wheel routing. Usage guidance lives in the gpux skill's [sticky positioning](../../../../../.claude/skills/gpux/references/sticky-positioning.md) and [programmatic scroll](../../../../../.claude/skills/gpux/references/programmatic-scroll.md) references.

## Current Technical Shape

`Position::Fixed` and `Position::Sticky` are first-class style variants exposed as `.fixed()` and `.sticky()` (generated by `gpui_macros`) with the existing inset APIs. `Style::to_taffy_style` maps fixed to absolute/out-of-flow and sticky to relative/in-flow. Fixed resolves insets against `Window::content_mask().bounds` (the viewport, or the clipped scrollport inside an overflow container) and keeps that mask for hitboxes, paint, and children. Sticky resolves each axis against the nearest scroll container context from `Window`'s frame-local context stack, which scrollable `Div`, `uniform_list`, and `list` push around descendant prepaint/paint, and clamps to the scroll content boundary.

Fixed and sticky divs paint through `Window::paint_positioned_layer` (a separate high draw-order range where each inserted primitive gets its own order) and register hitboxes through `Window::insert_positioned_hitbox` (high ids without reordering the stored hitbox vector); `Window::with_positioned_hitboxes` gives every hitbox inserted while a fixed or sticky subtree prepaints a positioned id too, so its interactive descendants hit-test in the layer they paint in. `Frame::hit_test` sorts only the hitboxes at the queried point before applying `BlockMouse`. Later overlapping positioned elements paint and hit-test above earlier ones. A fixed or sticky div reports its visual bounds through `Window::report_visual_bounds` while prepainting; `Drawable::prepaint` applies them to its devtools snapshot node (read by semantic locators) and its AccessKit node bounds and Click target, so tests and assistive technology see it where it paints. `Window::paint_top_layer(...)` and `Window::insert_top_layer_hitbox(...)` order above positioned content; scrollbars use them.

Sticky divs publish `StickyState`/`StickyAxisState` through `on_sticky_state_changed(...)` (stable id required; fires on first observation and x/y phase transitions only) and `when_stuck(...)` paint-oriented refinements. Candidates share the `StickyStacking` relation: `Level(u16)` for explicit hierarchy, `StackId(ElementId)` for one contiguous keyed run (a different id replaces the whole prior run; A-B-A starts a new run). `Window` discovers the full nearest-context candidate sequence before committing sticky bounds; unkeyed siblings derive flat or hierarchical lanes from equal or increasing start insets, and keyed siblings opt in with `sticky_stack_id(...)`. `StickyRelationIndex::next_replacement` returns the incoming candidate and retained ancestor depth; when it enters the outgoing suffix's combined primary-axis extent (height vertically, width horizontally), only that suffix is displaced, and root or keyed-run replacements push the whole chain. Active hierarchies traverse deepest-first in prepaint and paint, with incoming candidates appended last. `gpux::list` sums only measured rows into its scroll range, and a downward wheel that reaches the end of that range anchors the list to its last item, so a lazily measured variable-height list jumps to its end on the first wheel unless `measure_all()` or an item height hint keeps the range complete. `gpux::list` takes `StickyItem` through `with_sticky_items`/`set_sticky_items`, and `uniform_list` through `sticky_items`; plain indices mean `StickyStacking::Level(0)`, and both share the relation index with `div`. `VirtualList` (`elements/virtual_list/`, moved from RayX's `VirtualListBase` on 2026-10-16; `v_virtual_list`, `h_virtual_list`, `virtual_list`) renders items whose sizes are known up front through an `OrderedExtentIndex`, takes sticky rows as `VirtualStickyRow`s on the same relation index (rows with sticky metadata only are wrapped in a sticky root), and follows an external handle as `SynchronizedOffset` (it applies the offset itself) or `ParentViewport` (it takes its full extent in the parent's flow and renders what the parent's viewport shows). With scroll layers on, a vertical `VirtualList` that scrolls itself and has no sticky rows composites from a list layer (`fast::layers::lists::begin_virtual_list`, sharing `begin_rows` with `begin_uniform_list`); horizontal and sticky lists are counted `ineligible`, and external-scroll lists call no list hook.

Scrolling: GPUI owns the policy-neutral `ScrollBehavior::{Instant, Smooth(Duration)}` and one `ScrollActivity` state machine per scroll owner that tracks `Idle`, `Direct` (a held scrollbar thumb), and `Programmatic` activity; touch pans and their fling momentum arrive as ordinary `ScrollWheelEvent`s from Zed's touch recognizer. The programmatic driver applies or interpolates a resolved target at executor-clock timestamps (`BackgroundExecutor::now()`; instant and zero-duration requests use the timestamp-free `apply_immediate_programmatic`), retargets through a generation check, and schedules nothing after completion or cancellation; direct wheel, touch, thumb, selection, caret, and IME input cancels it immediately. `ScrollHandle` is the canonical owner identity (`ptr_eq`). `begin_scrollbar_drag`/`end_scrollbar_drag` bracket a thumb gesture so intermediate offsets stay `Direct` and settle to `Idle` on release. Concrete `ScrollHandle` thumb offsets publish in the pointer-input turn against the last accepted viewport and invalidate the owner and unique dependents once; other `ScrollbarHandleBase` implementations and layout and programmatic publications stay deferred.

Scrollable div wheel handling clamps the candidate offset against the current scrollport and stops propagation only when that scrollport actually moves. Interactive elements expose `capture_scroll_wheel` beside bubble-phase wheel handling; capture listeners run before nested bubble listeners on the same path, and `BlockMouse` remains a hit-test boundary.

Scroll layers (fast mode `scroll_layers`, `crates/gpui/src/fast/layers/`, adapted from gpui-fast): a scrolling `div` that scrolls on two frames in a row paints its content into a layer (a content-space scene, 512 px tiles, the viewport plus two viewports of overscan on either side) and frames that only scroll it insert tile sprites with reserved texture ids and carry the content's hitboxes, listeners, dispatch nodes, cursors, element states and tab stops, moved by the scroll and clipped to the viewport. A window runs them only where `COMPILED` (desktop, wasm32, Android and iOS) and `PlatformWindow::rasterizes_layer_tiles` hold, and their hooks without a window work only while the thread counts a window with them on (`layers::on_thread`); the wgpu renderer on Linux, Windows, the browser, Android and iOS and the Metal renderer on macOS do (see `renderer`), as does a test window without a renderer. A frame drawn from scratch drops every layer's record. `policy::decide` picks Composite, Repaint or Bypass per frame; every scroll-container frame is counted once in `FastStats` (`layers_composited`, `layers_repainted`, or `layer_bypasses` by reason).

A frame only scrolls a container when: nothing the content read changed; no content view was notified or rendered in the frame (a kept layout that could not stand); no nested container scrolled; the owner's render did not read the offset; the owner was not updated since it rendered (gpux counts an update without a notification as a change, so a scroll published from a `cx.listener` of the owner repaints) and was notified no more often than scroll state published (every publish, moved or metrics-only, credits the owner and `register_dependent_view` dependents: at once for a wheel or `publish_scrollbar_offset`, for the next frame when applied in prepaint; a `request_offset` the application notifies for repaints once); and no hover the content read can be true (every read hitbox is the content's own, was not hovered, is not captured, and at its new place is not under the pointer, so a wheel over a hover-styled row repaints). Publishes are logged per thread and each window takes those of the scroll states its own containers scroll by.

Carried listeners hold the bounds they were painted with, so input other than a scroll that reaches content shown away from where it was painted paints the layer first, in a frame drawn before dispatch (`input::before_dispatch`, `before_touch_gesture`): presses and releases anywhere, keys with focus inside, taps, long presses, touch-drag offers, and moves over the content where nothing covers it (the container's own hitbox counts; not the moves of a drag begun elsewhere or under a capture outside the content). While such input is dispatched (`WindowLayers::before_input`), any frame drawn, the one a dirty window draws before a key included, leaves no layer showing moved content. That frame also draws whatever else was pending, which without layers would be drawn after the input: the one timing difference layers make. Recognized touch scrolls and fling momentum are wheel input and composite. Ineligible content (deferred, anchored, children-prepainted listener, focused input, animation frame, visual effect, screen-effect region, HTML island or element, native surface, another composition surface, fixed, sticky or top-layer content; a rounded clip, a transform, a clip off device pixels, a background that is not one opaque quad) is drawn as without a layer and retried after 8 frames doubling to 2,048.

List layers (`fast/layers/lists.rs`): a `uniform_list` or `list` that scrolls on two frames in a row keeps its rows in a layer, one record a row (its part of the layer's scene, tile hashes, hitboxes, hovers, prepaint and paint ranges, layout keys, reads). A scroll-only frame (Extend) renders the rows the layer lacks, the rows whose hover a hitbox of the frame could change (`stale_rows`: a row that read hovers is kept only while each was false and of its own hitboxes, the pointer over none of them and none captured, so the row under the pointer is rendered every frame), the rows shown whose reads changed, and rows shown that were dropped or marked suspect; it carries the rest row by row. A `uniform_list` holds `ceil(0.5·viewport/item)` rows on either side (`paint::LIST_OVERSCAN_VIEWPORTS`; a div's layer keeps `OVERSCAN_VIEWPORTS` = 2); a `list` half a viewport on first paint, the rows shown after a content repaint, growing half a viewport per frame. Rows past the overscan drop a batch at a time (`retained_rows`, more than a quarter of the rows needed). Renderer tile caches release a tile no frame composited for `TILE_IDLE_FRAMES` (4) into a pool of `POOL_LIMIT` (4) textures, inside the 64/32 MB budget; `fast::tests::layer_memory` and the renderers' `tiles_no_frame_composites_are_released_after_a_few_frames` prove the bounds. The list places its rows by its item tree (`item_top` less the snapped scroll top); the layer places the rows around them by the same sum where every height between is measured (`anchor_rows`, `list_row_top`). Rows prepaint and paint in the rows' own space (`rows_region`), not inside the list's clip. The hovers of the rows kept and shown are added to the recordings around the list (`note_replayed_hovers` in `carry_paint_rows`), so the view holding it is drawn again once the pointer is over one. A row keeps the view holding its list among its reads; with scroll layers on, a row renderer's lease of that view is marked as drawing it (`dependencies::rendering_rows_of`, through `lists::render_items`). A `list` whose state version moved since it was painted without a wheel (`LayerRows::state_changed`) is not scroll-only: with the owner notified the rows shown are rendered again and the layer kept, otherwise the rows are painted afresh. Lists with sticky items and flipped uniform lists are counted ineligible.

A frame shown from a list's layer answers as the frame drawn without it: the rows held off screen keep their records in the frame for the next one to carry, but their hitboxes are clipped away, their mouse listeners are gated by a flag shared with the row (`listen_while_shown`), their tab stops and debug bounds are taken out of the lookups (`hide_records`), their focus ids out of the dispatch tree (`hide_focus`), their DevKit nodes flagged (`NodeRow`, left out by `shown_nodes`) and their hitboxes left out of the counts of the elements around them (`hidden_hitboxes`). The rows shown keep row order in the tab order (`order_tab_stops` lays a list's stops out again by row) and in the DevKit snapshot. The rows shown are those the list's own layout renders: a `list` notes them as it lays out and places (`keeps_row`'s `shown`, `place_list_item`; `flow` is the run from the first, `begin_placing` resets it for a second layout pass), a uniform list passes its visible range. A row lying across the bounds unshown (`hidden_in_view`: under padding) is neither held nor rendered. The row a `list` told of its rows' focus draws off screen (`RowsFrame::loose`) stays in the list's clip, is never held, and is painted into the frame after the layer is composited (`finish_list_paint`), last in the tab order, the DevKit snapshot and the description. A row off screen that asks for text input has the handler dropped and is not held (`RowPaint::unheld`). A row whose painted reach leaves its slot is remembered (`LayerRows::spilling`) and held only while shown; a row with a hitbox reaching outside its slot sends the list down the draw-into-the-frame fallback (`rows_overflow`), because the frame holds carried rows' hitboxes before rendered rows'; that fallback draws only the rows shown. `LayerRows::told` lists the rows with records in the frame last drawn, followed on frames that draw the owner from the last one; `fast/verify.rs` describes such a frame by the rows shown (`told_order`), leaving a record of a row off screen out only while it answers nothing (hitbox clipped empty, tab stop out of the order, focus not found, listener gated); `describe_frame_hit_at` adds what a pointer hits at given points.
