# Frame Pipeline Rules

- Preserve equal-key scene order for sprites. Use cached-key stable sorting only for the hot monochrome family; keep direct stable sorting for cold or empty families unless separate measurements justify the extra allocation path.
- Register arena drop records only when `std::mem::needs_drop::<T>()` is true.
- Keep `GlobalElementId` on direct `Arc<[ElementId]>` ownership with a cached `collections::FxHasher` hash and full-path equality. Keep reuse diagnostics hash-only behind `diagnostics`; do not add production interning without a measured design that improves throughput and tail latency.
- Keep the full accessed-state history for replay ranges and rollback; restrict finish-time removal to separately tracked replay keys.
- Keep deferred prepaint entries at stable arena indices; process them in place rather than moving completed entries behind cached ranges.
- Keep `gpux::deferred(...)` content-mask-preserving by default; use `Deferred::unclipped()` only for intentional top-level overlays.
- Preserve zero-width and zero-height content masks before device-pixel cover rounding; do not floor and ceil an empty mask independently.
- Elide inherited opacity transformation only when the factor is exactly `1.0`.
- Frame callbacks must not re-enter `AnyWindowHandle::update` to read app-owned platform snapshots; cache them through an owned subscription before scheduling frames.
- Remap inspection occurrence ids, parents, dispatch ids, focus, and property setters on cached prepaint replay, and truncate them on rollback.
- With view retention, copy a reused view's deferred draws with the records open at the copy, and stamp a reused record at the write generation of its reuse decision, never at its prepaint.
- Count an entity updated without a notify as changed for every reader that retained it; frames must equal the frame drawn from scratch.
- Splice only what can be copied exactly: refuse a deferred draw outside the gaps, check the copied content's reads and hovers before splicing, and keep a gap at its bounds; otherwise build the view and hand it the gaps as prebuilt.
- Everything a view's prepaint records per frame for composition (surface switches, native-surface placements) must be copied by every reuse path: `reuse_prepaint`/`reuse_paint`, retained `shifted` indices, and splice `StretchCopy`, and truncated by `transact`.
- A composition surface above native content takes pointer input only where it drew, never across the whole window.
- Whatever a scroll container's content adds to a frame while it is prepainted or painted must either be carried by `fast::layers::reuse` on composited frames or make the layer ineligible (`policy::eligible`, `paint::uncompositable`, `invalidate::note_uncarried`). A new primitive kind also needs `layers::scene` translation, `layers::tiles` hashing and `verify` expansion; a new per-frame record needs both.
- Gate scroll-offset snapping, glyph-origin quantization and path snapping on `WindowFastMode::snapping()`, never on `scroll_layers()`: a reference window snaps without layers (`Window::force_layer_snapping`), and with the switch off output must be today's.
- A layer composites only what one rectangular clip on device pixels can stand in for: gpux clips quads to the pixels covering a content mask and glyphs, underlines and paths to the mask itself.
- State an element inherits only while painting (opacity, transform, the surface or band it paints onto) is not seen by prepaint: record it with what was painted and, on a mismatch at reuse, show the old frame and rebuild on the next, as `retained::reuse_paint` and `layers::paint::repaint_if_painted_otherwise` do.
- Compare scroll layers against a window without them through `Window::painted_primitives` (tiles expanded into their content), frame by frame; a scenario or test window must put the platform pointer where its events are (`fast::scenarios::place_pointer`), because `draw_roots` reads it back from the platform window.
- A frame drawn while input other than a scroll is being dispatched must leave no scroll layer showing content away from where it was painted (`WindowLayers::before_input`): the window itself draws inside dispatch, before a key when it is dirty, and a composited pending scroll there hands the event stale listeners.
- Keep scroll-layer hooks inert without the switch: window-side hooks check `fast_mode.scroll_layers()`, hooks without a window check `fast::layers::on_thread()`, and a window turns layers on only where its renderer rasterizes tiles (`PlatformWindow::rasterizes_layer_tiles`, `PlatformHeadlessRenderer::rasterizes_layer_tiles`).
- Describe a frame that composites scroll layers through `layers::verify::expanded_scene` (what painting the content into the frame would have left), so `describe_rendered_frame` parity holds against a window drawn from scratch.
- At a clip's edge a scene orders primitives as with that clip not there, the other clips staying (`fast/edge_shadows.rs`): content in a clip must get, primitive for primitive, the draw orders it has unclipped, wherever the clip lies over it, because a blur is stacked among neighbours its box does not overlap by their draw orders, and tiles are painted once. Keep the parts together: the window tells the scene each clip (`push_clip`/`pop_clip` in `Window::with_content_mask`, `PaintOperation::FastClip`); an entry (bounds, order) per clip for each primitive the clip drops (`dropped`, also a `PaintOperation::FastDropped`), primitives a clip cuts raised past the entries they lie over (`raise`), a shadow that still draws inside its mask kept and ordered like a dropped primitive (`keep`). Never work from content masks alone: a mask is an intersection and does not say where an inner clip ends (the second review's blur over a clipped card) nor that a border has a mask of its own. Inside a container's content order nothing by a blur's reach: upstream orders by boxes, and a rule that raised what is painted over a kept shadow's blur made orders depend on whether the blur showed. What is painted after a scrolling container's content is ordered over every blur in it (`lay_over`, told by `Window::with_scroll_container_context`), because its layer is composited under whatever follows; enter each blur at the edges of the clips around the container too, or a sibling is raised only where the overlap shows (fourth review). Bound no part of the rule by a count of what was painted (clips, passes, primitives): a scene-wide limit on `behind` changed a shadow's order once sixteen bordered rows lay above the viewport, which is how far the content is scrolled. Nor let a clip cost a pass over the scene: `behind` asks `BoundsTree::max_behind`, and a primitive dropped out of reach leaves nothing at any clip (16 000 clipped cells went from 16 ms to 183 ms otherwise). Never share entries between clips because they clip alike: two containers laid over one another do; a clip goes on only when pushed again with nothing painted since it was popped. Change edge state only from what leaves a paint operation (a kept primitive, a `FastDropped`, a clip, a layer), or a replay diverges from the scene it replays. Never order a layer's content again in a scene that was not told its clips: keep its orders (`translated_scene`, `lift_paths`) or tell the clip (`replay_content`). Raise one past, not level (a rounding step in blending). `content_is_ordered_as_with_its_clip_not_there` and `content_with_clips_of_its_own_is_ordered_as_with_its_viewport_not_there` are the properties to keep. Anything that tells where a primitive draws (tile membership, tile hashes) uses `layers::scene::drawn_bounds`.
- A layer's tile draws its primitives at the content's orders (`LayerFrame::tile_scene`, `LayerContent::orders`), never at orders worked out among the primitives over the tile: per-tile orders change at a tile's edge and leave a step in a blur. A tile's hash covers the orders. Never order a list's whole content when a row comes or goes: `LayerContent::orders` orders only the parts whose extents meet, and keeps a part's orders with the part (the fifth review of s12c measured 13.7 ms a frame against 0.46 ms at the sprint's base and 1.4 ms with no layer). Order parts near the origin, not where they stand: a scene's trees keep everything past 16 384 px in their last cells. `a_list_scrolled_a_row_a_frame_orders_the_rows_that_come` and `rows_ordered_with_the_rows_they_meet_are_ordered_as_the_whole_content_is` hold both. A `list` paints its shown rows every frame: they keep the orders the painting scene gave them (`PaintPass`), but only a set all of whose rows were painted in that scene, in order, and that meets nothing else painted there, whether left out of the content as no part (`apart`) or as a part the content does not hold (`made`); lane C's `shadowed_list_rows_reaching_over_one_another_scroll_from_tiles` fails without the second check. Note what a pass leaves out in the space its parts stand in (moved by `to_content`). Find the sets without comparing every part with every set (`apart_in_order`, then `gather` by runs along an axis): a layer of 4 px rows holds 1300 of them. A part ordered as its operations are keeps no orders of its own (`PartOrders` none).
- A frame drawn from scratch (`window.refreshing`) drops every scroll layer's record (`policy::forget_content`): the renderer may have lost the sprite atlas the kept content names. Content painted after it takes a new generation.
- Drop every scroll layer's record when the window removes an entry from its sprite atlas (`layers::atlas_entry_removed`, from `drop_image` and `drop_svg`): the atlas hands the slot out again, and tile hashes name a sprite by its atlas tile.
- A scroll layer's content is everything the container scrolls over (`LayerRecord::content_bounds`: from the container's origin to its scroll extent, padding and child margins included). Cut the painted region and test coverage against the same bounds.
- Keep a view's render reads whole for whatever it built and cut at the last `list(...)` it built only for that list's rows (`dependencies::RenderReads::shaping`): cutting them for every scroll container the view holds leaves a scrolling `div` built after a list stale when what the view read in between changes.
- A lease that is part of drawing a view (`render_next`, `rendering_rows_of`) stamps neither an update nor a write; any new framework lease of a view while a frame is drawn needs the same mark, or every retained reader of the view is built again each frame.
- While scroll layers snap, a clip is grown to whole device pixels with `layers::paint::cover_bounds` (an edge within 1/64 px past a pixel's edge lies on it), an underline's by `cover_mask`, and a drop shadow's box is put on a 1/64 px grid (`shadow_bounds`): an edge is the element's device-pixel edge plus the scroll offset in logical pixels, off by a few millionths of a pixel by how far the content is scrolled, and a layer's content is painted at one offset and shown at others. Flooring took in a row more of a clipped card or an underline (hundreds of pixels), and an unsnapped shadow box moved its blur a level in a pixel or two, at every scale factor that is not whole. `lane_c_layer_scales` holds 1.25, 1.5 and 1.75.
- Content painted with a transformation of its own keeps its container off its layer (`paint::uncompositable`, `scene::transformed`): no tile reproduces interpolated positions to the last bit.
- What an element with a hover style was painted as is what a pointer's move is compared with (`fast/hover.rs`, hooks in `Interactivity::paint_mouse_listeners`), in every mode. Upstream writes that state only when a move changes it, so a hover the wheel changed stayed on screen when the pointer went back to the row it had been over; fast mode's retained rows repainted by the hovers they read and did not, which is how the two differed. Ask a hitbox whether it is hovered only for an element that has a hover style: the question is a hover read, and what is retained is painted again for it.
- An empty content mask is the clips told (`edge_shadows::told`): `cover_bounds` does not grow an empty box, the clips told are grown, and at scale factors whose edges fall between pixels the two differ by millionths of a pixel; taken for an untold clip it stopped `levels` and a dropped shadow left no edge (first review of s12d). Never compare a primitive's mask with the clips told by equality alone.
- The 1/64 px of `cover_bounds` holds to about 50 000 device pixels from a container's origin at fractional scales (an `f32` edge is origin plus offset); a `uniform_list` holds to 300 000. Snapping alone differs from no snapping by a row at a clip's edge. A hover is compared only for an element with an id.
