# Frame Pipeline Hypotheses

- Fresh global-element-id arc allocation and broad `Window::draw` orchestration remain top sampled costs after the 2026-08 loop; a design that avoids per-frame path allocation without interning state may pay off, but small inline storage is already rejected by the depth distribution.
- While a scroll layer's content is painted, `Window::content_mask()` is the painted region (viewport plus overscan), not the viewport. `list` takes its scrollport from the content mask and gpui-mobile's `platform_view_element` places its native surface by it: check both when lists get layers and when layers compile on mobile.
- Removing an image or SVG from the sprite atlas drops every scroll layer's content in the window, so an image cache that evicts while a list scrolls keeps that list's layer from compositing. Dropping only the layers whose content names the removed tile, or hashing something that identifies a tile's content, would keep the others.
- A list whose rows all draw outside their room (a shadow under every card) renders each new row twice with a layer: once ahead, where it is found spilling and dropped, and once when it shows. Remembering that a list's rows spill and skipping the overscan for it would halve that; unmeasured.
- Rows a list's layer carries keep their hitboxes and mouse listeners ahead of the rows it renders, not in row order. `rows_overflow` keeps lists whose rows can be hit outside their slots off layers, and listeners are gated per row; carrying rows in row order as the list places them would make the frame's order the list's and lift both.
- A list row first rendered into a layer's overscan has its autoscroll request discarded (`end_list` takes it), so an element that consumes a pending autoscroll request as it prepaints loses it when the row shows from the layer. Keeping the request with the row, or rendering a row with one when it shows, would close it; no test shows it yet.
- A list with a focused text input in a row it shows stays off its layer (a layer whose record holds an input handler is ineligible), and at scale 1.1 a list with 8 px of padding never took its layer in the second sprint 12 review's probes; both are untuned, not wrong.
- Ordering at a clip's edge is exact where the scene is told the clips (`Window::with_content_mask`, a layer's painted region, the viewport of a replay). A list's rows are painted in the regions of the frames that painted them, pushed onto the window's stacks directly, and `LayerContent::orders` replays them into a scene told none of those: a row's primitive cut by its region inside a clip of its own (a card across the region's edge) is taken as painted in one clip, which is approximate there. Telling each row's region in its part would close it. A border costs four clips: eight paint operations and four sets of entries, about a fifth of the time to insert a page of bordered cells (third review of s12c). Painting a border's four quads in one clip, or telling the scene a clip only once something in it is cut, would spare most of it.
- A dropped primitive's entry costs about 0.1 µs, so a long page of text scrolled without a layer pays for every glyph within 2048 px of the viewport (24 000 quads: 7.1 ms against 4.9 ms at a reach of 4096). Entering dropped primitives only when a later primitive of their mask is cut or kept would spare what lies below the viewport.
- Telling clips and keeping entries cost scenes painted whole 10 to 56 % over what they cost before the kept shadows (eighth review of s12c, its probe: 8000 bordered rows 12.3 to 19.1 ms, a table of 2000 rows by 20 cells 30.9 to 38.4 ms). Most of the 19.1 was empty masks taken for untold clips, closed in s12d (`told`); a border told as one clip (s12e, `border_clips`) took a quarter of the operations of a bordered page. Same machine, optimized, best of 7, 8000 bordered rows painted whole: before the kept shadows (`8285288`) 9.7 ms; with a clip told for each strip 11.2 to 12.4 ms; with one clip for a border 10.6 to 12.0 ms (64 770 operations to 16 770); a table of 2000 rows by 20 masked cells 24.5 ms before, 27.1 now. What remains is the clip record and the question asked of each dropped quad. Keeping a clip's first entry in the clip record, and making its set of entries only for a second, would spare the sets of the clips that cut one thing each.
- `scene::keep_painted` looks at every part its pass made for each set, so a `list` showing n rows compares n² extents a frame outside `COMPARED_EXTENTS` (about 30 000 for 173 rows, a few hundredths of a millisecond). A row with a quad at a non-finite or absurd origin (NaN, minus infinity, -1e9 px) that meets its neighbours is ordered otherwise than the whole content painted in one scene, and -1e30 px trips the debug assertion in `ordered_together`; no layout makes such bounds, and leaving non-finite bounds out of `ordering_extent` would close the first.
- Edges worked out from a scroll offset in logical pixels carry a few millionths of a device pixel of error at fractional scale factors (more far down a long container: an `f32` holds a 100 000 px offset to about a hundredth of a pixel). `paint::cover_bounds` and `shadow_bounds` absorb it for clips and shadows while scroll layers snap; anything else that floors, ceils or rounds a device coordinate at an exact half (a new primitive, a stroke placed by its centre) would show it again as a row of pixels. Applying the scroll offset in device pixels, after scaling, would remove the error at its source.
- Applying the scroll offset in device pixels, after scaling, would remove the far-offset limit of `paint::cover_bounds` and the 1/64 grid altogether (about 50 000 device pixels at fractional scales), at the price of changing how every element offset is applied. A layer's tile ids run out at about 1.05 million device pixels (`layer_tile_id` asserts in a debug build). A hover style on an element with no id is compared with nothing (upstream's): recording the painted hover by hitbox would close it.
- A fully rounded element with a border (`rounded_full`: radius half its height) at a layout position that is not a binary fraction takes the clip for each strip in about 12 % of cases: its top and bottom strips touch, and an ulp in `top + reach` against `bottom - reach` makes `border_clips::inside` see them overlap. The scene is the same and the cost the old one; a tolerance of a hundredth of a pixel in `inside` would take them (third review of s12e).
- Text (glyphs) inside a rotated or scaled element differs from the reference by a few pixels of one level (1 to 7 pixels, up to 5 levels: `[250]` against `[255]` along a glyph's stem) in a window with every mechanism, not translated text, at scales 1 and 2 and not 1.5, in lane C: scroll layers with retained layout and retained views together (mechanism bits 1011 and 1111) and neither without the other, no layer composited (`Ineligible`); a frame-description comparison (primitives, bounds, masks, orders) of rotated text rows shows no difference, so it is below the scene (glyph sprites' placement or subpixel variant, text carry). Found by adding a transformed card clipping underlined text and a path to `lane_c_layer_scales` (the third content of the transformed groups; the s12f reviewer's probe variant 7, a differently built card, was equal). Not a defect of the mask repair (`transformed_clip`); to be diagnosed in a corrective task before the epic closes. Reproduce: rows of `div().mx_3().h(44).px_2().bg(white).border_1().overflow_hidden().text_size(12).rotate(0.03)` holding one line of text, scrolled by `scrolls_and_single_pixels` and a long scroll, scale 2, `FastMode { layout_retention, view_retention, scroll_layers }`.
